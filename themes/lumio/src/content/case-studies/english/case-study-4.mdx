Vongo was the industry's first portable subscription video service. I conceived the product, pitched it internally, secured the $12M budget, built the team from scratch, and shipped it on time at the CES 2006 keynote — where it was presented by Bill Gates.

## What Was Different About Vongo [.text-h4]

In 2005, streaming video was not yet a category. The dominant model was broadcast and packaged media. Vongo's premise — that a subscriber could pay a monthly fee and download Starz content to a laptop for offline viewing — required solving problems that had no existing playbook.

That meant:
- Integrating 23 vendors from scratch: encoding, CDN, DRM, billing, and device management
- Negotiating studio approval for a distribution model that studios had never seen before
- Building a production-grade, DRM-enforced platform at scale with zero critical defects
- Launching to the largest consumer electronics audience in the world at CES with the product working

## Results [.text-h4]

- **1 million downloads in 9 months**
- **3 million downloads in 36 months**
- **Emmy nomination** for technology innovation
- Established the template for portable subscription video that the industry subsequently followed

Vongo predates Netflix streaming by two years and the App Store by three. The architecture decisions made in 2005 — how to think about content licensing windows, device portability, and DRM enforcement — remain structurally relevant today.
